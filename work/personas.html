Building Personas through Thick Data

Context

By 2021, CreditBook's digital ledger had attracted 300,000 monthly active businesses across Pakistan, but the company's growth was unsustainable without a path to revenue. Given that roughly 60% of the country's SMEs rely on informal financing, we saw an opportunity to finance the businesses already using the app regularly. Since lending is culturally sensitive — and religion shapes beliefs about financial products — we set out to understand who we were actually building for before designing a lending product. I applied data analytics and ethnography together: analysing the financial data of power users to identify behavioural clusters, drawing on phenomenology to conduct 50 in-depth interviews, and building six personas to guide the lending strategy.

Problem

"How might we identify app users that are willing and able to adopt a digital financing product to grow their business in the informal economy?"

Hypothesis

Active users who record credit transactions frequently are most likely to adopt digital financing products to grow their business.

Clustering

We cleaned and analysed data to group users by time since active, transaction frequency and size, location, feature usage, and financial literacy — arriving at six main clusters based on business size, tech adoption, financial habits, and risk tolerance. We repeated the exercise after six months to track how clusters shifted.

Interviews & observations

Inspired by phenomenology, I led a team of three user researchers through in-depth observations of six users across each cluster, aiming to understand their needs, motivations, and daily behaviors. We conducted in-depth interviews, made sense of the data through affinity and empathy mapping, and identified patterns across age, financial behaviours, family systems, and belief systems.

Personas

  • Sumbul, 28 — a driven entrepreneur managing a small general store in Karachi, an early tech adopter who trusts CreditBook but keeps paper backups out of meticulousness.
  • Fatima, 31 — ambitious tailoring-business owner constrained by family responsibilities, tech-savvy but unsure about formal banking.
  • Salma, late 50s — runs a grocery store with her children's help, values free, easy tools over growth or expansion.
  • Zain, 33 — confident, risk-taking garment-shop owner who expanded through informal financing and social platforms.
  • Ali, 40 — store manager with entrepreneurial ambitions held back by past failures and limited resources.
  • Ahmad, 37 — runs several small side businesses, values affordability and steady income over rapid growth.

Conclusion

We arrived at six relatable personas, each with a story, traits, and motivations — inspired by Grounded Theory and the concept of "thick data" to turn raw insight into stories that resonate with both CreditBook's mission and its customers' needs. Ultimately, the personas helped us understand who to approach — and who not to — for our first lending pilot.
